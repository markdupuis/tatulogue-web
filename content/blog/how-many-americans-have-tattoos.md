People in their 30s and 40s lead, women outpace men, and lower-income adults are tattooed far more often than upper-income ones. Two newer polls landed closer to 25%, so treat any single number as a range.

---

Count the forearms next time you're in line for coffee. Somebody has ink. Probably more than one somebody.

So how many people have tattoos, really? It depends who you ask and how they asked. Every "X% of Americans" headline you've seen traces back to a survey with a specific sample, a specific question, and a specific month.

We went to the source. Pew Research Center's 2023 survey is the most detailed national look we found, and it's worth reading past the headline. We also opened two newer polls, from late 2024 and early 2025, that give lower numbers.

Here's what the data says, what it doesn't, and how to read it without fooling yourself. We'll cover who gets tattooed, why they say they did it, and how many wish they hadn't.

---

## So how many people have tattoos?

About one in three US adults, according to the best-known recent survey. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/08/15/32-of-americans-have-a-tattoo-including-22-who-have-more-than-one/) found that 32% of adults have at least one tattoo, and 22% have more than one. That means roughly 11% have exactly one.

Pew surveyed 8,480 adults from July 10 to 16, 2023, using its American Trends Panel, which it recruits through random sampling of residential addresses. Pew's [topline document](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/SR_23.08.15_Tattoos_topline.pdf) lists a margin of error of 1.5 percentage points for the full sample. The exact question was whether you currently have any tattoos.

Pew also notes that a 2010 phone survey put the figure at 23%. Pew itself says that older result is not directly comparable, because the survey mode and question wording changed. So don't build a "tattoos jumped nine points" story on it.

## What stands out in the numbers

**The peak is 30 to 49, not the youngest group.** Under-30s sit at 41%, but adults 30 to 49 hit 46%. Our guess, not Pew's: collections take years and money to build. The drop after 50 is steep, from 25% at 50 to 64 down to 13% at 65 and up.

**Women lead men by 11 points.** Women are at 38%, men at 27%. Among women under 50 it's more than half.

**Income runs the opposite way you might guess.** Lower-income adults (43%) are tattooed at about double the rate of upper-income adults (21%). Education follows the same direction. Pew reports the numbers but doesn't explain why, and we won't pretend to.

**Place barely matters.** Pew says roughly a third of people in urban, suburban and rural areas have a tattoo. If you picture tattoos as a big-city thing, the data disagrees.

## Why do people get tattoos?

Most tattooed adults say it's about someone or something they want to keep. Among tattooed adults, 69% told Pew they got a tattoo to remember or honor someone or something. For 47%, a major reason was exactly that.

Pew asked about three motives, and each respondent could say yes to more than one:

| Reason | Share of tattooed adults (major or minor) |
|---|---|
| To remember or honor someone or something | 69% |
| To make a statement about what I believe | 47% |
| To improve my personal appearance | 32% |

Women were more likely than men to cite remembering or honoring someone (73% versus 63%). Pew found the "statement about beliefs" motive was highest among Black tattooed adults at 56%.

Notice what's at the bottom. Looking better is the smallest of the three reasons Pew offered. Keep in mind Pew gave respondents only these three options. A different list could have produced a different ranking.

## Do people regret their tattoos?

Most don't. Pew found 24% of tattooed adults say they ever regret getting a tattoo, which means 76% don't.

The question was whether you "ever" regret your tattoo or any of your tattoos. That's a low bar. Someone who loves nine pieces and winces at one blurry first tattoo counts as a yes. Pew doesn't tell us how strong the regret is, whether it led to removal, or which piece was the problem.

By group, Pew reported about 30% for Hispanic tattooed adults, 23% for White and 21% for Black tattooed adults. Among those with a bachelor's degree or more, it was 19%.

## What about the people without tattoos?

Among adults with no tattoo, Pew found 85% say they are not too or not at all likely to get one. Another 9% say somewhat likely, and 6% say extremely or very likely.

Age matters here. Among untattooed adults under 30, 19% said they were extremely or very likely to get one. That falls to 7% at 30 to 49, 2% at 50 to 64, and 1% at 65 and up.

Attitudes are a separate question. Of untattooed adults, 66% said seeing a tattoo gives them neither a positive nor negative impression of the person. 29% said more negative than positive and 5% said more positive. Among all adults, 80% said society has become more accepting of tattoos over roughly the past 20 years.

## Do newer surveys agree with Pew?

Not exactly. Pew's survey is from 2023, so we looked for anything newer. We found two polls we could open and check. We did not find a newer Pew tattoo survey.

| Survey | Fieldwork | Sample | Share with a tattoo |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/08/15/32-of-americans-have-a-tattoo-including-22-who-have-more-than-one/) | July 10 to 16, 2023 | 8,480 US adults, random-address-recruited panel | 32% |
| [AARP, run through Ipsos](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/social-leisure/body-modifications-tattoos/) | December 12 to 19, 2024 | 3,076 US adults, Ipsos KnowledgePanel | 25% |
| [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52074-americans-have-become-more-favorable-toward-tattoos-in-past-decade-poll) | March 31 to April 2, 2025 | 1,115 US adult citizens, opt-in online panel | 24% |

YouGov reports a margin of error of about 4 points. AARP's page didn't report one.

Both newer numbers are lower than Pew's. Does that mean tattoos got less popular in a year or two? We doubt it, and nothing in these sources says so. Different panels, different weighting, and different wording can easily move a result by several points. Pew itself says even its own 2010 and 2023 numbers can't be lined up.

YouGov's age breakdown also doesn't line up neatly with Pew's. It groups people differently, and it found younger adults at 25% versus about a third of adults 30 to 44. Even so, both surveys put the 30s and 40s at the top. And YouGov found women at 30% and men at 19%, so the gender gap shows up there too.

## How to read these percentages without fooling yourself

Short version: treat "32%" as "somewhere around a quarter to a third, depending on the survey."

- **It's self-reported.** Nobody checked skin. People answered a survey question, and Pew's wording was whether they currently have any tattoos.
- **It's one survey per number.** Pew 2023 is one snapshot, and the other two are separate snapshots with different methods.
- **Subgroups are noisier.** Pew's 1.5 point margin applies to the full sample. Numbers for a smaller slice, like women under 30, carry a bigger margin. Pew said the sample of tattooed Asian Americans was too small to analyze for some of its questions.
- **"At least one" hides a lot.** A single small tattoo and a full bodysuit both count as yes. These numbers say nothing about size, style or placement.
- **Percentages of adults only.** None of this covers teenagers.
- **Correlation isn't explanation.** Pew shows who is tattooed, not why those groups differ.

## What we take from it

Our own read, not a Pew finding: the "tattoos are mainstream" line is true, but it's too tidy. The data shows a big, varied crowd. It's not one demographic, it's not one city, and it's not mostly about looking cool.

The reasons are the part that sticks. In Pew's data, most tattooed adults say they got one to hold on to someone or something. That's worth remembering when you're picking a design.

---

## Conclusion

Roughly a quarter to a third of US adults have a tattoo, depending on whether you trust Pew's 2023 number (32%) or the lower late-2024 and 2025 polls (about 25%). Either way, you are not an outlier.

The bigger takeaways: the 30 to 49 group is the most tattooed in Pew's data, women lead men, lower-income adults are tattooed more than upper-income adults, and most people with ink don't regret it. In Pew's list, memory and meaning rank above appearance as a reason.

## FAQ

### How many people have tattoos in the US?

Pew Research Center found in July 2023 that 32% of US adults have at least one tattoo and 22% have more than one. Later polls were lower: AARP, run through Ipsos, found 25% in December 2024, and YouGov found 24% in spring 2025. Differences in method make a range of roughly 24% to 32% the honest answer.

### Who is most likely to have a tattoo?

In Pew's 2023 data, adults ages 30 to 49 are the most tattooed age group at 46%, and women are more likely than men (38% versus 27%). Women ages 18 to 29 and 30 to 49 were at 56% and 53%. Lower-income adults (43%) were also tattooed more than upper-income adults (21%).

### Are tattoos more common in cities than in rural areas?

Not in Pew's survey. It reported that roughly a third of adults in urban, suburban and rural areas have a tattoo. Geography did not show up as a big divide in that data.

### What percentage of people regret their tattoos?

Pew found 24% of tattooed adults say they ever regret getting a tattoo or any of their tattoos, and 76% do not. The question covers any regret about any tattoo, so it doesn't measure how serious the regret is or whether anyone had one removed.

### Have tattoos become more common since 2010?

We can't say from Pew's data. Pew reported 23% in a 2010 phone survey, but it says that result is not directly comparable to its 2023 online survey because the mode and question wording changed. The two numbers should not be treated as a clean trend line.
