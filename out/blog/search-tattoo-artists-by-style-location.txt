8:T7ea,<p>Finding a tattoo artist usually means scrolling Instagram hashtags, digging through shop websites, or asking around and hoping someone's recommendation actually matches what you want. None of that is really search. It's guessing with extra steps.</p>
<hr>
<h2>The Problem With Finding Artists the Old Way</h2>
<p>Instagram's discovery tools weren't built for this. Hashtags surface whatever's popular that week, not what's near you or in the style you actually want. Shop websites are inconsistent, some have portfolios, some don't. And word of mouth only covers the artists your friends happen to know.</p>
<h2>How Search Works on Tatulogue</h2>
<p>Tatulogue's search is built around the three things that actually matter when you're looking for a tattoo artist: style, location, and content.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Style</strong>: filter by tags like neo-traditional, fine line, black and grey, and more, instead of hoping the right hashtag surfaces the right work.</li>
<li><strong>Location</strong>: search artists near you, set a location radius, or search by city, state, or zip if you're planning to travel for the right artist.</li>
<li><strong>Content</strong>: search users, or search the actual content they've posted, so you're browsing real work, not just profiles.</li>
</ul>
<p>You're not sifting through an algorithm's idea of what's trending. You're looking for the thing you're actually trying to find.</p>
<h2>Why This Matters</h2>
<p>A search built around style and location means the artist you find is more likely to actually be a fit, someone whose work matches what you're picturing and who's realistically bookable near you. It also means artists doing great, specific work don't need a viral moment to get seen by the right collector.</p>
