<hr>
<p>Some artists say books are slow, walk-ins are down and shops are closing. Others say the industry is crashing.</p>
<p>Then you read the other side and it's market-research headlines about a booming tattoo economy. Both camps usually skip the part where they show their numbers.</p>
<p>So we went looking for numbers. Real ones, from sources you can open yourself. Here's what's solid, what's soft, and what nobody has measured. This is the business side. For what collectors are getting, see <a href="/blog/tattoo-trends-2026">tattoo trends 2026</a>, and for style definitions, the <a href="/blog/tattoo-styles-guide-2026">tattoo styles guide</a>.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Is the Tattoo Industry in a Recession in 2026?</h2>
<p>Not on any observed data we could find. The best official numbers show tattoo-inclusive businesses and jobs still rising through early 2026, but growth has slowed hard. Private estimates show a flat 2025. Artists report slow books. That's a cooling and a crowded market, not a documented collapse.</p>
<p>One thing up front. We're not using "recession" in a technical sense. We mean one simple question: is the business shrinking? Shops closing, jobs dropping, spending falling.</p>
<p>The honest answer is that a few signals point to a stall, one official source says still growing, and nobody tracks tattoo shops cleanly enough to settle it.</p>
<hr>
<h2>What Does Federal Payroll Data Show?</h2>
<p>The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts employer businesses by industry code. Tattoo parlors sit inside "Other Personal Care Services," and that category kept growing through the first quarter of 2026: 34,197 establishments, up from 32,840 a year earlier. Growth is slowing, though.</p>
<p>Here's the catch, and it's a big one. That code is a mixed bag. It includes <a href="https://www.ibisworld.com/classifications/naics/812199/other-personal-care-services" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">tattoo parlors</a> next to day spas, tanning salons, massage parlors and hair removal. We can't tell you what share is tattoo shops.</p>
<p>It also only counts businesses with employees on payroll. The BLS program <a href="https://www.bls.gov/cew/overview.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">excludes proprietors and the self-employed</a>, and plenty of tattooers are booth renters. So it undercounts the way a lot of artists actually work.</p>
<p>With that said, here are the <a href="https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2025/a/industry/812199.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS figures</a>, private sector, annual averages:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>2019:</strong> 22,213 establishments, 147,940 jobs</li>
<li><strong>2023:</strong> 29,933 establishments, 172,176 jobs</li>
<li><strong>2024:</strong> 31,986 establishments, 181,112 jobs</li>
<li><strong>2025:</strong> 33,417 establishments, 187,817 jobs</li>
</ul>
<p>Our math on those numbers: establishment growth ran about 10% in 2022 and 2023, then 6.9% in 2024 and 4.5% in 2025. Employment grew 3.7% in 2025. In the <a href="https://data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/2026/1/industry/812199.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">first quarter of 2026</a> it was still up about 4% on establishments and 3% on jobs compared to a year earlier.</p>
<p>That's a boom cooling off. It's not a crash. Jobs per business also slid from about 6.7 in 2019 to about 5.6 in 2025, which fits "more, smaller businesses." But because the category is mixed, we can't prove that's tattoo shops.</p>
<hr>
<h2>What Do Private Industry Estimates Say?</h2>
<p>IBISWorld's US tattoo estimates point to a stall rather than a collapse. It puts revenue at $1.3 billion, with "no change in 2025 alone," and the number of businesses at 23,774 in 2025, down 1.8% from 2024. These are a research firm's estimates, with the method behind a paywall.</p>
<p>That's the closest thing to a "recession" signal we found, and it's mild. Flat revenue plus a small drop in businesses is a plateau. <a href="https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/tattoo-artists/4404/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IBISWorld's US page</a> also says revenue has climbed at a 10.9% compound annual rate through the end of 2025 (it doesn't say over which years), so the long run is still way up.</p>
<p>Be careful with it. It's a firm's model, not a census. Its business count doesn't match the BLS figure above, most likely because the two count different things. Treat it as a second opinion that leans "flat," not a hard number.</p>
<hr>
<h2>What Are Artists Actually Reporting?</h2>
<p>Artists describe a crowded market with higher costs, and some say it's their slowest stretch in years. CBC News reported in July 2025 that Montreal tattooers see too many artists chasing the same clients. That's real reporting from working artists. It's also one city and a handful of voices.</p>
<p>In that <a href="https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/montreal/tattoo-market-oversaturated-1.7587782" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CBC story</a>, one artist said he'd never seen a slowdown like it in 12 years. Another said supply and demand have "kind of met," meaning too many artists. CBC also notes Quebec doesn't regulate the trade, and cites asking rents in Montreal up nearly 71% since 2019.</p>
<p>Notice what that says. It's not "nobody wants tattoos." It's more artists, higher costs, same pool of clients. Even the artist who said it was slow told CBC the industry is evolving, not dying. A packed market feels like a recession from inside a half-empty book.</p>
<p>If you're booking right now, <a href="/blog/how-to-vet-a-tattoo-artist">how to vet a tattoo artist</a> matters more when there are more artists to pick from.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Where Does the "Over 50% Drop" Number Come From?</h2>
<p>It traces back to a single unsourced sentence on a studio's blog. <a href="https://www.theblackhattattoo.com/blog/tattoo-market-shift-structure-vs-volume" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Black Hat Tattoo</a> wrote in April 2025 that client flow "has dropped by over 50% in the last two years" across Europe and beyond. The post cites no study or data for it.</p>
<p>Then it traveled. <a href="https://xactbodyart.com/our-blog/slow-season-for-tattoo-shops-crisis-why-the-tattoo-industry-recession-is-hitting-artists-hard-in-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Xact Body Art's November 2025 post</a> repeats it and attributes it to Black Hat. A tattooer's own "recession or evolution" post lists both as sources. That's how an opinion turns into a "stat" after enough reposts.</p>
<p>The "booming market" side has its own problem. Research firms publish forecasts, and they disagree wildly. <a href="https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/tattoo-market-104434" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fortune Business Insights</a> puts the global market at USD 2.43 billion in 2025 and projects 10.67% annual growth to 2034. IBISWorld puts the US alone at $1.3 billion. One unsourced stats blog says $4.2 to $4.8 billion for the US in 2026. A forecast isn't observed data, and numbers that far apart tell you nobody is counting.</p>
<hr>
<h2>What Can't We Say?</h2>
<p>A lot. We couldn't find a tattoo-only count of shops opening and closing, state license numbers across the country, convention attendance reports, a survey of artist income, or a search-interest trend. Nothing we found measures bookings or hours in the chair. Those would settle this.</p>
<p>We also can't say what happened after March 2026. The BLS hadn't published second-quarter numbers for this category when we checked.</p>
<p>And we can't say demand is flat or falling. The best survey we found is <a href="https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/08/15/32-of-americans-have-a-tattoo-including-22-who-have-more-than-one/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Pew's 2023 poll</a>, which found 32% of US adults have a tattoo. That's one snapshot, with no trend we can use.</p>
<p>So if someone tells you the industry is dying, or booming, ask for the source. Then check what it actually counts.</p>
<hr>
<h2>What Does This Mean If You Get Tattooed or Tattoo?</h2>
<p>If you collect, the practical effect is more choice, and you should use it carefully. A slow market doesn't make a cheap quote a good deal. See what <a href="/blog/how-much-do-tattoos-cost">tattoos actually cost in 2026</a> and what <a href="/blog/tattoo-trends-2026">collectors are booking</a>.</p>
<p>If you tattoo, the data backs a less dramatic read. More shops and more artists competing for clients, with growth slowing. That's a different problem than a market that vanished, and it has different fixes. We won't pretend we have a magic one.</p>
<p>We'll update this when real numbers show up. Until then, we'd rather tell you what we don't know than hand you a scary chart.</p>
<hr>
<p>Here's where it lands. The industry doesn't look like it's in a documented recession. It looks like a fast-growing trade that got crowded and hit a plateau, with some artists getting squeezed hard. The official numbers say still growing but slower. The private estimates say flat. The shop owners say tough. All three can be true.</p>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<p><strong>Is the tattoo industry in a recession in 2026?</strong>
We found no observed data showing one. BLS payroll data for the category that includes tattoo parlors shows more establishments and jobs through the first quarter of 2026, though growth has slowed. IBISWorld estimates US revenue was flat in 2025. There's no clean tattoo-only count to settle it.</p>
<p><strong>Are tattoo shops closing?</strong>
Individual shops close, same as in any trade. But we found no complete count of tattoo-only closures. BLS shows a net rise in establishments in the broader category through early 2026, while IBISWorld estimates the number of US tattoo artist businesses fell 1.8% in 2025.</p>
<p><strong>Is the tattoo market really growing 10% a year?</strong>
Research-firm forecasts say so. Fortune Business Insights projects 10.67% annual growth from 2026 to 2034, and IBISWorld says US revenue has climbed at a 10.9% compound annual rate through 2025. Those are projections and estimates, not observed counts, and IBISWorld had 2025 revenue flat.</p>
<p><strong>Do fewer people want tattoos now?</strong>
We couldn't verify that. Pew found 32% of US adults had a tattoo in a July 2023 survey, but we found no comparable recent survey, so there's no trend to cite. Pew itself says its 2010 figure can't be directly compared.</p>
<p><strong>Why do artists say it's slow if the data shows growth?</strong>
Growth in the number of artists and shops can outpace growth in clients, so each artist gets less. Artists in a CBC Montreal report pointed to too many artists and higher costs. Our data can't prove the cause, and the BLS code includes non-tattoo businesses.</p>
