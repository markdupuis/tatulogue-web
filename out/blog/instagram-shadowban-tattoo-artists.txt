<p>Getting shadowbanned. Every tattooer has a theory about it. The healed back piece that pulled nothing. The thigh Reel that sat at 200 views while the flash sheet next to it went wide.</p>
<p>So here's the plain version of the Instagram shadowban: what causes it on posts and Reels, what Instagram actually calls it, and how to check if it's happening to your account. No guru tricks. Just what Instagram's own pages say, and how that lands on tattoo work.</p>
<p>We already covered the bigger reach problem in <a href="/blog/instagram-failing-tattoo-artists">why Instagram is failing tattoo artists</a>. This one is narrower. It's about the stuff that gets an account flagged, and the stuff you can actually fix.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="1600" height="2400" style="max-width:min(100%,373px)" src="/images/blog/instagram-shadowban-tattoo-artists.jpg" alt="Tattooed hand holding a phone on a clip mount, framing a photo of a client&#x27;s black and grey wolf sleeve on the screen"></p>
<hr>
<h2>Is the Instagram shadowban real?</h2>
<p>Sort of. Instagram doesn't use the word. Its official term is content or accounts that are "not eligible for recommendation." Your followers can still see your posts, but Instagram stops pushing them to people who don't follow you, in Explore, Reels, Search and suggested accounts.</p>
<p>Adam Mosseri, the head of Instagram, addressed the word directly in a 2021 post, <a href="https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/shedding-more-light-on-how-instagram-works" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Shedding More Light on How Instagram Works</a>. He wrote that people often accuse Instagram of "shadowbanning" or silencing them, and that it's a broad term people use for a lot of different experiences. He also said Instagram can't promise you'll reach the same number of people every time you post.</p>
<p>Then Instagram built a tool that basically answers the question. On its <a href="https://help.instagram.com/653964212890722" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Recommendation eligibility</a> help page, Instagram says professional accounts can see in Account Status whether their content may be recommended to people who don't follow them. It also says that if your account isn't eligible, none of your content gets recommended, and your posts can still show up for your followers.</p>
<p>So call it whatever you want. The mechanism is real, it has a name, and you can check it.</p>
<h2>What causes an Instagram shadowban on tattoo posts?</h2>
<p>Mostly skin. Instagram's <a href="https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Recommendations guidelines</a> list content that's allowed on the app but may not be recommended, and "sexually explicit or suggestive" content is on that list, with pictures of people in see-through clothing as the example. Tattoo photos show a lot of skin, so that's where most tattoo accounts get caught.</p>
<p>Think about where big work lives. Chest pieces. Thigh pieces. Hip, ribs, butt, sternum. Instagram's own examples of content it hides from teens include near nudity covered by a digital overlay and a photo zoomed in on someone's buttocks. A back piece shot with the client topless and a sticker over the chest is close to that first example.</p>
<p>The removal rules are stricter. Meta's <a href="https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/adult-nudity-sexual-activity/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Adult Nudity and Sexual Activity standard</a>, which covers Instagram, doesn't allow visible female nipples (outside contexts like breastfeeding or protest) or close-ups of visible buttocks. It limits images to adults 18 and older when the crotch, buttocks or female breasts are the focus of the image. There's one tattoo-specific carve-out: visible female nipples shared in a medical or health context, including mastectomy tattoos, get a sensitive content label instead.</p>
<p>What about blood, needles and fresh redness? We went looking. Nothing we read in Instagram's guidelines names tattooing, tattoo blood or fresh redness. The violence category in the Recommendations guidelines gives people fighting as its example. What Instagram does say is that it uses technology to detect content and accounts that don't meet its guidelines. That's a machine reading skin, not a tattooer.</p>
<p>What you can control:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Crop to the piece.</strong> If the tattoo is the point, make the tattoo fill the frame, not the body part around it.</li>
<li><strong>Lose the overlays.</strong> Stickers and emoji over nudity are close to the "near nudity" example Instagram gives.</li>
<li><strong>Watch your profile too.</strong> Instagram says it avoids recommending accounts that repeatedly put content it won't recommend in the profile photo, bio or name.</li>
<li><strong>Post healed shots.</strong> Healed work reads better anyway. Our <a href="/blog/tattoo-healing-stages">tattoo healing stages</a> guide covers when a piece is actually settled.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Banned and broken hashtags</h2>
<p>Some hashtags just stop working. Instagram's help page on <a href="https://help.instagram.com/485240378261318" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">why you can't search for certain hashtags</a> says a hashtag may not be searchable if the text or the content tied to it consistently breaks its Terms of Use or Community Standards. Instagram reviews those tags periodically and can turn them back on.</p>
<p>So if a tag you've used for years suddenly leads nowhere, that's probably not you. It's the tag. Body part hashtags and anything that pulls in adult content are the obvious risks for tattoo accounts. Use style and city tags people actually search, like the style name plus your town, and drop any tag that comes up empty.</p>
<h2>Engagement bait, giveaways and pods</h2>
<p>Instagram's creator page on <a href="https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-Originality" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">helping creators find new audiences</a> lists "content that users broadly tell us they dislike, such as clickbait or engagement bait" as not eligible for recommendations. "Comment FIRE if you'd get this" is engagement bait. So is "tag 3 friends."</p>
<p>Giveaways need care. Meta's <a href="https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Spam standard</a> doesn't allow offering anything of monetary value in exchange for engagement, and its example is literally liking a page to get entered to win. A "like, share and follow to win a free tattoo" post is close enough to that line that you should read the policy before you run one.</p>
<p>Engagement pods count too. The Recommendations guidelines name "coordinated comment networks intended to artificially drive engagement and distribution" as low-quality publishing. Ten artists dropping fire emoji on each other's posts within a minute is exactly that.</p>
<h2>Bot-like behavior and third-party apps</h2>
<p>Follow-unfollow apps, auto-likers and anything that runs your account for you are a straight risk. Instagram's <a href="https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Terms of Use</a> say you can't create accounts or access or collect information in an automated way without Instagram's express permission.</p>
<p>Meta's Spam standard goes further. It doesn't allow posting, sharing or engaging with content at very high frequencies, whether by hand or automatically. It also says Meta may restrict accounts acting at lower frequencies when other spam signals show up, like repetitive content.</p>
<p>Buying followers or likes is its own problem. The Recommendations guidelines say Instagram tries not to recommend accounts that repeatedly used misleading practices to build a following, such as purchasing likes. You pay for fake numbers and lose the real reach. Bad trade.</p>
<h2>Reposting other artists' work and watermarks</h2>
<p>Posting other people's tattoos is a fast way out of recommendations. In April 2024, Instagram <a href="https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-Originality" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">announced</a> that accounts that repeatedly post content from other users that they didn't create or meaningfully improve would not be shown where Instagram recommends content. When it finds identical copies, it recommends only the original and labels the copy with a link back to the original creator.</p>
<p>The same announcement says eligible content has no visible watermarks. If you cut a Reel in another app and post it with that app's watermark still on it, you're working against yourself. Export clean.</p>
<p>This is also where stolen portfolios come from. Fake artist accounts built on other people's work are a real scam in this industry, and we broke down how to spot them in <a href="/blog/tattoo-scams-fake-artist-accounts">tattoo scams and fake artist accounts</a>. Post your own work. Credit collabs properly.</p>
<h2>Reports, strikes and repeat violations</h2>
<p>Reports send your content to review. If it's removed, that's a strike on your record. Instagram's <a href="https://help.instagram.com/539126347315373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Account Status page</a> says repeatedly posting content that gets removed for breaking Community Standards can cost you access to your account, and that in most cases you'll get a warning first.</p>
<p>Removals also hit reach. The Recommendations guidelines say Instagram tries not to recommend accounts that recently violated its Community Guidelines. One removed post can pull the whole account out of Explore for a while.</p>
<p>There's a flip side. Instagram's Terms of Use also say people can't misuse the reporting system with fraudulent or groundless reports. If a rival is mass-reporting your work, request a review on every removal you think was wrong.</p>
<h2>Copyrighted audio on Reels</h2>
<p>Music on Reels is a rights issue, not a vibe issue. Instagram's page on <a href="https://help.instagram.com/402084904469945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">access to the licensed music library</a> says the library is meant for personal, non-commercial use, and that certain business accounts and certain types of posts don't get access to it. Licensed music may also be unavailable in some countries.</p>
<p>If your shop account can't use popular tracks, that's why. Instagram points those accounts to Meta's Sound Collection, which it describes as over 14,000 royalty-free songs and sounds that are safe to use in Reels and Stories. We didn't find an official Instagram page saying a song choice by itself pulls a Reel out of recommendations, so we're not going to claim it does. Licensed audio from Instagram's own library or Sound Collection keeps you clear of the rights question.</p>

<h2>How to check for an Instagram shadowban: Account Status</h2>
<p>Go to Settings, then Account Status. On a professional account, it shows whether your content may be recommended to people who don't follow you, flags anything in your profile that breaks the Recommendations guidelines, shows a sample of posts that can't be recommended, and lets you edit, delete or request a review.</p>
<p>That's straight from Instagram's <a href="https://help.instagram.com/653964212890722" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Recommendation eligibility</a> page. It also shows removed content and any features you've lost. If something's flagged, fix it or appeal it. If nothing's flagged and reach is still down, you're not shadowbanned. You're just in a slow stretch, and that's the bigger problem we covered in <a href="/blog/instagram-failing-tattoo-artists">why Instagram is failing tattoo artists</a>.</p>
<p>One catch. Instagram says eligibility for recommendations doesn't guarantee your content will be recommended. A clean Account Status means you're allowed in the game, not that you'll win.</p>

<h3>Does Instagram shadowban tattoo artists?</h3>
<p>Instagram doesn't use the word shadowban. It says some content and accounts are "not eligible for recommendation," which means posts still reach followers but aren't shown to non-followers in Explore, Reels, Search or suggested accounts. Tattoo accounts often hit this because of skin that reads as sexually suggestive.</p>
<h3>How do I know if my Instagram account is shadowbanned?</h3>
<p>Check Account Status in Settings. On a professional account, Instagram shows whether your content may be recommended to people who don't follow you, a sample of posts that can't be recommended, and anything in your profile that breaks its Recommendations guidelines. You can edit, delete or request a review from there.</p>
<h3>Why are my tattoo Reels not getting views?</h3>
<p>Common causes are content read as sexually suggestive, engagement bait, visible watermarks from other apps, reposting work you didn't create, and recent Community Guidelines violations. If Account Status shows nothing flagged, the drop is likely normal reach variation, which Instagram says it can't promise to keep consistent.</p>

<p>Instagram says some hashtags aren't searchable if the text or the content tied to them consistently breaks its rules. Posting with those tags won't get you found through them. Instagram reviews these hashtags periodically and may make them searchable again.</p>
